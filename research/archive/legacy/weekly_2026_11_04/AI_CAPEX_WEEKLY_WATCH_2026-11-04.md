## Memory cycle, Micron and Cisco-2000 mechanics

Micron reported FY revenue $133.2bn, Q4 gross margin 80.7%, Q4 OCF $44.0bn, FY CapEx $27.4bn and guided next-quarter gross margin near 86%. SK hynix says over half of 2027–28 capacity is covered by LTAs with 14 customers and price bands; Samsung sees 2027 demand above supply. TrendForce expects Q4 conventional DRAM +10–15% QoQ and NAND +15–20%.

This is **not** Cisco 2000/2001 yet. The sequence has reached orders/capacity acceleration, but the confirming steps are missing: broad inventory build, cancellations, collapsing bookings, rental-price erosion, sell-through failure and supplier-margin compression. Current evidence is the opposite on pricing and margins. The risk is late-cycle valuation and capacity response, plus possible double ordering hidden by long-term agreements.

Micron is a **peak-earnings trap risk** even while the physical cycle is strong. Current P/E capitalizes extraordinary 80–86% gross margins and cannot represent normalized cyclic earnings. A normalization toward prior-cycle 40–50% gross margins would reduce earnings far more than revenue, so valuation must use through-cycle cash and margins. This is a scenario framework, not a forecast or a new target price.

## AI ROI / Payback — six required answers

1. **Is AI demand growing? Yes.** Cloud growth, accelerator revenue, GPU executed prices, HBM contracts and supplier results all confirm it.
2. **Is monetization keeping up with capital deployment? No at enterprise cash-flow level.** All five fixed issuers show watch-level CapEx/revenue gaps and weaker FCF conversion, despite strong cloud segments.
3. **Is payback lengthening? Early evidence, not systemwide proof.** Anthropic compute intensity worsened sequentially; CoreWeave has shorter customer contracts than debt maturity; Oracle relies on prepayments and costly issuance. Comparable project IRR/payback cohorts remain unavailable.
4. **Is financing becoming more necessary or expensive? Yes.** Oracle, Meta, Amazon, CoreWeave and project SPVs use more debt/equity/prepayments; market access remains open, so this is dependence rather than funding break.
5. **Are marginal projects delayed, restructured or cancelled? No qualified material event by cutoff.** Conditions, unfunded announcements and unleased capacity are risks, not cancellations.
6. **Is financial stress transmitting to utilization/pricing/hardware? Only to equities/technicals.** GPU rental prices and hardware/memory demand remain firm; no confirmed utilization or hardware-order weakness.

Capacity funnel: announcements and grid/lease commitments are expanding faster than proof at ENERGIZED→UTILIZED→MONETIZED→ECONOMICALLY_PROFITABLE. The observable bottleneck is economic verification, not yet physical availability. China property is useful only as a causal analogy for pre-selling/funding dependence and duplicated demand; AI assets have shorter hardware lives, more global reusable compute and faster price discovery, so the analogy does not establish identical outcomes.
